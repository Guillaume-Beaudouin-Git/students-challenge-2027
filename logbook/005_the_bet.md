## Decision

Inside the equity sleeve, which is 30% of the portfolio at the open:

- the six largest AI companies (NVIDIA, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Broadcom, Meta) held together
  at **half their index weight**: 9.2% of the sleeve against 18.3% in the index;
- **three lines of 1.5% of the portfolio in regulated electricity networks**: 15% of the sleeve,
  against 2.5% for all utilities in the index.

The bet takes no view on the direction of the market: the total held in shares is set by the rule
(Entry 001).

## The cash

Four of the six buy the AI build-out (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta). Two sell it to them
(NVIDIA, Broadcom).

- In the quarter to September 2026 the four invested $202bn (capital expenditure, not all of it
  for AI), $26bn more than their operations brought in. In the same quarter a year earlier they had
  $62bn left.
- Over four quarters: $677bn of operating cash flow, $615bn of capital expenditure, $264bn of net
  borrowing. What is left is 0.6% of their market value.
- Their accounts charge 3.5 times less in depreciation than they spend, so reported earnings do not
  show it yet. On reported earnings the six are priced for growth of about 3% a year for ten years;
  on the cash they keep, for 19% a year (discount rate of 8.5%, growth of 2.5% afterwards).

## The logic

Cash reserves and borrowing can fill that gap for a time, and the spending may pay. If the gap is
closed by spending less, the two suppliers lose orders. The market has charged the sellers far more
than the buyers: 42% against 14% in the AI correction of the autumn. The index holds 18% in the six.
We hold half. The net borrowing is what their cash-flow statements report for the period; we do not
claim that it was raised for AI.

## The other leg

A data centre needs a grid connection, paid for by regulated tariffs whatever the AI budgets. Our
basket is eight companies whose business is regulated electricity networks and which, on our
reading of their reports, own no coal-fired plant: Exelon, Consolidated Edison, Eversource,
National Grid, Terna, Redeia, E.ON, Hydro One. We would hold three of them.

They are not cheap, and they behave like long bonds:

- 18 times earnings (median), a dividend of 3.3%, 1.6 points under the ten-year Treasury yield;
- capital expenditure of 125% of their operating cash flow;
- since 2023, about 0.7% lost for each rise of 0.10 point in the ten-year yield.

We count them as interest-rate risk: they lengthen our bonds from 2.5 to about 3 years.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| −3.4 | −2.2 | −0.1 | −1.4 | +1.1 | +0.6 |

- Since 2016 the bet has lost money in three of every four three-month periods. The worst cost
  1.3% of the portfolio, the best earned 1.7%.
- About 40% of its gain in the autumn came from holding less market risk (the bet lowers the
  sleeve's exposure to the index by about 15%).
- After the four earlier falls of 20% of the six, the bet lost twice and gained twice over the
  next three months.
- After our cut-off: on Monday 23 November the six rose 12% and the bet lost 0.35% of the portfolio
  in a day. Measured to the close of 24 November, the AI correction reads −18% for the six and
  +0.9% for the bet.

## Objection

*Tom: capital expenditure is an investment. If it pays, cash flow recovers, and at 18 times
earnings the six are not expensive. We would be late, after a fall of 25%.*

Alexandre: that is why we hold half the index weight and not none, and why the test is dated. The
four report between late January and early February, around our first order.

## Wrong if

- Results of late January and early February 2027: the four report cash left over with spending
  still near $200bn a quarter. The spending pays: we go back to index weight.
- If they cut spending instead, their cash recovers and their suppliers lose orders: the
  underweight stays on NVIDIA and Broadcom only.
- The ten-year Treasury yield passes 5.31%, its high of 2026: the networks are halved.
- The bet is down 0.5% of the portfolio from 2 February: halved. Down 1%: closed, and this entry
  says what we misread.

## Definitions

- Capital expenditure: cash paid for property and equipment, as in each company's cash-flow
  statement. Finance leases are not included.
- Net borrowing: net issuance of debt in the same statements over the last four reported quarters,
  in billions of dollars: Microsoft −8, Alphabet +97, Amazon +124, Meta +51. Total: 264.
- Networks: regulated operators of electricity networks. Schneider Electric, the stock example of
  entry 002, is an equipment maker and sits outside this basket.
- The six: the four largest buyers of AI computing capacity and their two largest suppliers in the
  index. The basket is ours and is not a ranking by size. Apple, the largest line of the index, and
  Tesla stay at index weight: they spent $9bn and $15bn on capital expenditure over twelve months,
  against $104bn to $215bn for each of the four.
- Six times: 15% of the sleeve against the 2.5% of all utilities in the index. The eight network
  companies alone weigh at most 0.36% of it (full market values; the index counts the free float).
- Eight and three: the eight are our research universe and the basket behind the history above.
  The three lines will be chosen among them, one entry each, on ESG rating, valuation and
  liquidity. Their return will differ from the basket's.
- What the figures measure: two tilts, each against the index. They are not the return of the
  whole equity sleeve. With the sleeve completed (no other utility, the rest held like the rest of
  the index), the figures move by 0.26 point of the portfolio at most: −3.1 in place of −3.4 for
  2023, and less than 0.1 elsewhere. What our own stock picks add or cost is counted apart.
- The yield sensitivity of the networks, −0.7% for each 0.10 point, has a 95% interval of −0.9 to
  −0.5 with standard errors that allow for serial correlation. It is a historical association
  over four years and no forecast.
