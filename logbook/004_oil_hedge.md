## Decision

**5% of the portfolio in an exchange-traded commodity on energy and metals,** in two lines. 10%, in
three lines, if Brent passes $110. No oil producer.

## Evidence

- Oil shock, 27 February to 31 March 2026: a broad commodity basket gained 15.3% while developed
  equities lost 5.7% and 7–10-year Treasuries 2.3%.
- AI correction, 6 October to 20 November 2026: the same basket lost 10.3%.

This is a hedge for one scenario. It costs money in the others.

## What ESG changed

We want the price of oil and none of a producer's reserves or drilling budget: hence an
exchange-traded commodity with no agricultural contract. No standard counts the carbon of such a
position, so it stays outside our carbon figure, in plain sight, and every committee pack reports
what the choice costs: the return of our line less that of the energy sector of the index.

## Objection

*Juliette: it still profits from dearer oil.*

Tom: yes. It owns no reserves, it is capped at 10% of the portfolio, and it has its own line in
every pack, never netted against the rest.

## Open question

Whether an exchange-traded commodity on energy and metals is eligible. We ask at the kick-off on
8 January 2027.

## Wrong if

Brent under $80 for a month: the line goes to zero.
