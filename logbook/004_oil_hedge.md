## Decision

**5% of the portfolio in an exchange-traded commodity on energy and metals,** in two lines. 10% if
Brent passes $110. No oil producer.

## Evidence

- Oil shock, 27 February to 31 March 2026: a broad commodity basket gained 15.3% while developed
  equities lost 5.7% and 7–10-year Treasuries 2.3%.
- AI correction, 6 October to 20 November 2026: the same basket lost 10.3%.

This is a hedge for one scenario. It costs money in the others.

## What ESG changed

An oil major would hedge the same shock and pay a dividend. It would also spend our carbon budget:
we commit to keep the carbon intensity of the equity sleeve 30% below the MSCI World's, and energy
is the most carbon-intensive sector of that index. So the hedge is an exchange-traded commodity,
and every committee pack reports what the choice costs: the return of our line less that of the
energy sector of the index.

## Objection

*It still profits from dearer oil.*

Answer: yes. It finances no producer, it is capped at 10% of the portfolio, and it has its own
line in every pack, never netted against the rest.

## Open question

Whether an exchange-traded commodity on energy and metals is eligible. We ask at the kick-off on
8 January 2027.

## Wrong if

Brent under $80 for a month: the line goes to zero.
